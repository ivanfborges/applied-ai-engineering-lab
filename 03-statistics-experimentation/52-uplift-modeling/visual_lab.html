01 / Counterfactuals

Why can't we directly observe an individual treatment effect?

One person faces two possible actions at the same decision point. Random assignment reveals one binary outcome. The other stays missing, even in an experiment. The response probabilities shown below are known only because this person is synthetic.

Observed Y = T·Y(1) + (1−T)·Y(0)   |   CATE(X) = μ₁(X) − μ₀(X)
↙   Two hypothetical branches   ↘

Animated assignment and counterfactual missingness

What to observe

Probability μₜ is a conditional expectation. The realized Y(t) is 0 or 1. Assignment reveals one realization.

Common misconception

A randomized experiment does not reveal both outcomes for each person. Subtracting observed outcomes across different people is not an individual effect.

Production implication

Define the decision, treatment version, outcome window and randomization unit before collecting labels.
02 / Response versus effect

Why do response and uplift rankings disagree?

Four illustrative segments have different control and treatment response probabilities. The treatment-strength control changes only the hypothetical treatment probabilities. Empirical uplift estimates come from one fixed, independent synthetic segment experiment at strength 1; they do not update as if a new trial had run.

Response ranking: μ₀ or μ₁   |   Effect ranking: τ = μ₁ − μ₀

What to observe

The already-loyal segment has high response under both actions. Persuadables have the largest positive change; harmed customers have negative uplift.

Common misconception

Here “response propensity” means conversion probability. Treatment propensity e(X)=P(T=1|X) is a different probability.

Production implication

A churn or conversion model alone cannot identify who benefits from an offer. Preserve a comparison population.
03 / Effect geometry

What does CATE look like as a surface?

Engagement and price sensitivity are standardized pre-treatment features. Control and treatment surfaces live on probability scales; CATE is their vertical difference at exactly the same coordinates. Rotate and zoom each 3D chart. Cameras stay synchronized across the three views.

μ₀ = sigmoid(L₀); μ₁ = sigmoid(L₀ + s·h); τ = μ₁ − μ₀
Exact synthetic response functions

L₀ = −0.3 + 0.85·X₁ − 0.65·X₂ + 0.25·sin(X₁X₂)
h = 0.9·tanh(X₂) − 0.7·tanh(X₁) + 0.5·sin(X₁X₂). Features are uniform on [−2.5, 2.5]. No clipping is needed to bound these probabilities.

Reading the scales

Blue = positive effect, red = negative effect, cream = zero. Every effect view uses the same zero-centered −1 to +1 probability-point scale. Control/treatment probabilities share 0–1 axes.

This control changes an analytical thought experiment only. The fitted models and holdout used in Modules 4, 6 and 7 remain fixed at strength 1.

What to observe

At strength 0 the probability surfaces coincide and CATE is zero everywhere. Large absolute response need not imply a large gap.

Common misconception

A CATE surface is not a predicted binary outcome or a guarantee for any individual. Camera rotation does not represent time.

Production implication

Transporting heterogeneity estimates requires comparable feature support and stable treatment definitions.
04 / Meta-learners

How do S- and T-Learners construct counterfactual contrasts?

Both learners use the same randomized training sample and the same forest settings. The S-Learner fits one model with treatment as a feature; the T-Learner fits separate arm models. True CATE is available for evaluation only in this simulation.

S-LEARNER (X, T) → one model μ̂(X,T) Predict μ̂(X,0) and μ̂(X,1) Subtract → τ̂S(X)
T-LEARNER Control rows → model μ̂₀(X) Treatment rows → model μ̂₁(X) Subtract → τ̂T(X)
τ̂ − τ = (μ̂₁ − μ₁) − (μ̂₀ − μ₀) = ε₁ − ε₀
Watch the subtraction of predicted surfaces

What to observe

Each outcome-model error enters the contrast. The displayed RMSE is against known test CATE, not an observed individual-effect label.

Common misconception

Neither learner is universally better. S can ignore treatment; T divides data between models. One seed does not establish a ranking of methods.

Production implication

Use honest evaluation, sufficient observations per arm and pre-treatment features. Classifier accuracy alone is not effect validation.
05 / Sample size and uncertainty

Why are small subgroups so unstable?

Repeated synthetic segment experiments estimate a difference in independent binomial proportions. Each total sample is divided across four segments and two treatment arms. No forest is fitted here: the point is sampling uncertainty before model complexity.

τ̂b = Ȳ₁,b − Ȳ₀,b   |   approximate pointwise 95% Newcombe score intervals
How are the intervals calculated?

Compute a two-sided 95% Wilson score interval [Lₜ,Uₜ] for each arm proportion p̂ₜ. Newcombe's unpooled difference interval has lower bound d−sqrt((p̂₁−L₁)²+(U₀−p̂₀)²) and upper bound d+sqrt((U₁−p̂₁)²+(p̂₀−L₀)²), d=p̂₁−p̂₀. These are approximate pointwise intervals, not simultaneous coverage guarantees. The dashed reference is the known segment-population effect. Observed coverage across these replications is descriptive and has Monte Carlo error.

What to observe

Total N is not subgroup effective N. Read the actual treated and control counts. Larger samples usually narrow intervals, but individual replications need not improve monotonically.

Common misconception

A 95% confidence interval does not assign 95% probability to the fixed true effect, and many subgroup intervals are not jointly 95% reliable.

Production implication

Plan sample sizes for decision-relevant segments and uncertainty. Do not discover and validate segments on the same outcomes.
06 / Ranking evaluation

Does the ranking concentrate incremental outcomes?

A frozen T-Learner ranks an independent randomized holdout. Observed outcomes and the known assignment probability e=0.5 evaluate targeting. The expected random line uses the same holdout endpoint. The curve can fall when more people are included.

Ĝ(q) = (1/n) Σselected [T·Y/e − (1−T)·Y/(1−e)]   |   denominator = all eligible people

Exploring q here is educational. Selecting a policy after seeing this holdout would require a fresh final test sample.

What to observe

All curves start at zero and meet at the IPW ATE at 100%. Gains are per eligible person; multiply by 1,000 for outcomes per 1,000 eligible people.

Common misconception

AUUC is the integral of the uncentered gain. Our Qini area subtracts Ĝ(1)/2, the random-line area. It is unnormalized and may differ from a library coefficient.

Production implication

Compare operationally relevant frozen policies with uncertainty, valid assignment logs and mature outcomes. One curve is not a deployment result.
07 / Costs and decisions

When is an intervention worth its cost?

The policy treats up to the capacity limit, in descending estimated CATE order, only when estimated incremental value is strictly positive. Monetary inputs are illustrative currency units. All contacts cost money, including unsuccessful ones.

π = top-capacity rows with v·τ̂ > c   |   V̂inc = (1/n) Σ π·(v·Z − c)

Grid uses the holdout score cutoff. Boundary ties are resolved by input order in the cohort.

Animate the cost threshold

What to observe

Predicted, IPW-estimated and true synthetic values answer different questions. The policy can leave capacity unused. Treat-none has zero incremental value by definition.

Common misconception

Positive uplift is not necessarily positive profit. True synthetic value is an oracle diagnostic, not a production observable.

Production implication

Uniform values/costs permit threshold plus ranking. Heterogeneous costs and a budget can require constrained allocation; the present rule does not solve that problem.
08 / Identification and overlap

When can apparently convincing estimates mislead?

Compare random assignment, observed-feature confounding and poor overlap using the same response functions. Adjusted estimates use the known synthetic assignment probability, without fitted nuisance models or weight clipping. Each regime has repeated independent datasets.

e(X) = P(T=1|X); Z = T·Y/e − (1−T)·Y/(1−e); ESSₜ = (Σwₜ)² / Σwₜ²

What to observe

Observed-feature assignment changes the two populations. Large inverse probabilities give rare observations high influence. Repeated IPW errors show how poor support affects variability.

Common misconception

IPW does not remove hidden confounding. Poor-overlap propensity is bounded at 0.001/0.999 by the data-generating assignment rule; this is not a repair applied to the estimator.

Production implication

Defend exchangeability, consistency and interference assumptions. Inspect arm-specific ESS and support before trusting a causal targeting model.
09 / Applied AI decisions

How does uplift become a measurement system?

Consider proactive AI-assisted case processing. Predicting a difficult case, predicting improvement from AI assistance and deciding whether that improvement justifies cost are three different tasks. This workflow is conceptual; no production system or LLM effect was measured.

Difficulty: P(failure|X)   →   Incremental success: τ(X)   →   Decision: v·τ̂(X) > c

What to observe

The loop includes outcome maturity, policy evaluation, eligibility and assignment logs, not just a prediction endpoint.

Common misconception

An LLM recommendation is not a causal estimate. A model's confidence does not establish the effect of acting on its recommendation.

Production implication

Randomized monitoring preserves comparisons only where its design supports both actions. Log total assignment probabilities, including the mixture of policy and exploration.
